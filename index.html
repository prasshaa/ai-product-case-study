01 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The opportunity was bigger than “predict the right code.”

Arintra automates medical coding inside the healthcare revenue cycle. The opportunity here was to extend automation into procedure CPT coding — translating documented medical procedures into standardized billing codes used downstream in claims.

Because procedure coding can depend on clinical context, procedure details, modifiers and coding rules, the product had to solve two problems simultaneously: coding quality and trustworthy automation.

My ownership

Product decision layer

Roadmap and business case, evaluation methodology, defect analysis, reliability, release gates and the strategy for moving from human-reviewed AI assistance toward autonomous processing.

Engineering owned the deep implementation of the underlying models and pipeline.

The product thesis: maximum safe automation — not maximum automation at any cost.

CPT

A standardized system of procedure codes used to describe medical services for billing.

Why procedures are hard

The right code can depend on anatomy, technique, quantity, guidance, laterality and clinical context.

Why AI matters

A coder is reasoning over unstructured clinical evidence, not simply looking up a word in a table.

03 · PROBLEM STATEMENT

How do we automate a financially consequential workflow without treating model confidence as proof?

ProblemWhat it meant for the product
Limited coding intelligenceThe starting workflow largely passed through procedure codes supplied by the EHR, with limited validation.
Clinical contextCorrect procedure coding can require information embedded in the clinical note rather than the existing code.
ComplianceProcedure coding needs controls such as NCCI, MUE, bundling and modifier logic.
Human review burdenIf AI cannot be trusted, coders remain the safety net — limiting automation value.

The product problem: create enough coding intelligence to expand coverage while creating enough evidence and control to know when the system is safe to act autonomously.

05 · PRODUCT STRATEGY

Autonomy should be a graduation process, not a launch switch.

01

Bound the scope

Start where procedure volume and customer value are meaningful, then expand from evidence.

02

Separate AI from rules

Use probabilistic reasoning for clinical ambiguity and deterministic controls for compliance-critical logic.

03

Graduate by segment

Use CPT × specialty × customer × setting rather than one global quality number.

North star
Increase the share of the workflow that AI can handle without increasing unacceptable coding risk.
06 · PRODUCT / MVP SCOPE

Two distinct technical approaches emerged around the same product problem.

The important nuance: this was not a controlled MVP1-vs-MVP2 experiment. MVP1 never reached a measured production state.

MVP 1 · Apr–Jun 2026

Multi-AQM CPT Engine

Decompose clinical interpretation into many narrow AI extraction tasks, assemble the evidence, then determine CPTs and validate them.

Never merged to main
MVP 2 · Jul 2026 onward

MAC Pipeline

Classify, retrieve evidence, reason, critique, revise within a bounded loop, adjudicate and then apply deterministic validation.

Production architecture

MVP 1 — Multi-AQM

Pass 1

Identify

Pass 2

Enrichment

What Multi-AQM means: a set of narrow, independently scoped LLM extraction calls organized into dependency-aware passes. It is not inherently multi-agent; there is no agent-to-agent negotiation or critique loop in the evidenced design.

07 · APPROACH & TRADEOFFS

Which approach won?

Operationally, MVP2 won because it is the architecture that shipped. But there is no evidence for the stronger claim that MVP1 was experimentally defeated by MVP2.

MVP1 has no measured production accuracy, latency, cost or failure-rate evidence in the available source material. So the honest product conclusion is about architecture and operationalization — not an apples-to-apples benchmark winner.

DimensionMulti-AQMMAC Pipeline
ReasoningMany narrow extraction specialistsRole-based reasoning + bounded critique
KnowledgeStructured extractionRetrieval-grounded reasoning
CorrectionNo critique loop evidencedReviewer + bounded revision
ComplexityMany schemas and dependenciesMore centralized reasoning orchestration
ComplianceDeterministic controls designedDeterministic controls live
EvidenceNo production benchmark foundProduction-era evaluation available
Product tradeoff: decomposition gives explicit, inspectable questions; reasoning agents give more flexibility but introduce new failure modes in retrieval, reasoning and orchestration.

09 · EVALUATION SYSTEM

Trust was a system, not a single accuracy score.

I defined evaluation across the full AI lifecycle: offline to decide whether a release is good enough, inline to stop unsafe individual decisions, and online to detect whether quality holds after deployment. Deterministic guardrails sit across the runtime path wherever the answer is knowable without model judgment.

01 · OFFLINE

Is the system actually good?

Run against a fixed/versioned test set before release and whenever prompts, models or rules change.

Core gates: precision / recall / F1, case exact match, hallucination, total misses, tier accuracy, modifiers, high-$ / RVU performance, confidence calibration, reproducibility and regression.

Implemented / measured
02 · INLINE

Is this individual decision safe?

Run synchronously inside the pipeline and block or reroute the specific chart when a hard condition fails.

Core gates: evidence grounding, NCCI Indicator-0, MUE, non-billable checks, mutual exclusivity and combined-code rules; confidence routing once a reliable score exists.

Runtime guardrails
03 · ONLINE

Does quality hold in production?

Evaluate live traffic retrospectively without blocking the current chart, with the ability to trigger investigation or rollback.

Defined: shadow-mode agreement, canary drift monitoring, customer-reported misses and review-queue burden.

Framework defined · infra gap identified
04 · DETERMINISTIC

Where should AI not make the decision?

For compliance and documentation-derived logic with an authoritative rule, use deterministic logic instead of asking an LLM to judge it.

Examples: NCCI, MUE, non-billable status and other rule-based suppression / validation.

Cross-cutting safety principle
“Offline tells me whether the system is good. Inline tells me whether this decision is safe. Online tells me whether those assumptions continue to hold in the real world.”

That distinction became important because the production system had an agentic reasoning architecture, but the online shadow/canary infrastructure required for continuous production evaluation was not yet confirmed to exist.

The important product decision: I did not treat “95% accuracy” as the definition of readiness. The release question became whether the system was accurate, evidence-grounded, compliant, observable and sufficiently reliable at the exact CPT × specialty × customer × setting unit being considered for autonomy.
10 · WHAT THE EVALS ACTUALLY FOUND

The score was less useful than the failure modes.

Diagnosis

Context blindness

64615/64616 errors exposed a static order-text → CPT mapping that could ignore diagnosis context; 64616 recall was 22.2% in the cited analysis.

Mapping

Duplication / remapping

11402 showed 10.4% precision; 15850/15851 also showed confirmed precision issues that drove fixes.

Modifiers

Sequencing complexity

Modifier analysis exposed a genuine modifier-25 / E&M interaction that drove product and sequencing work.

A single “AI accuracy” number would have hidden the actual work required to make the system safe to automate.

The ground-truth problem

A raw comparison against historical coding labels can incorrectly blame the model when the reference is incomplete or questionable. The evaluation therefore used five verdicts: GT-questionable, engine overcode, engine miss, insufficient evidence, and wrong specificity.

In the cited sample, 37.7% of judge verdicts were GT-questionable. That made evaluation methodology itself a product problem.

13 · WHAT I WOULD BUILD NEXT

The next step is controlled expansion — not simply removing the human.

01

Calibrate confidence

Connect confidence to observed correctness by CPT × specialty × customer × setting.

02

Prioritize high-impact gaps

Focus on high-$ / RVU cases, diagnosis-aware coding, modifiers and recurring regression clusters.

03

Graduate deliberately

Use shadow/online evaluation and evidence gates before expanding T0.

Current operating-state context

The newer stated operating state is that 75% of eligible procedure charts within the covered release are autonomously processed, with the remaining 25% routed to human review or blocked because they do not meet confidence/data-quality requirements.

This newer operating state should not be conflated with the historical repository snapshot, where confidence was not populated and routing remained T1.
